# Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF) — October 2026 Reference

Source: U.S. Department of Education, Federal Student Aid, “Public Service Loan Forgiveness,” accessed November 16, 2026.
https://studentaid.gov/manage-loans/forgiveness-cancellation/public-service

This note summarizes high-level FSA guidance. PSLF eligibility is fact-specific. Borrowers should use the PSLF Help Tool and StudentAid.gov to verify their own loans, employer, qualifying-payment count, and employment certification.

## Core PSLF framework

PSLF is available for eligible Direct Loan borrowers after 120 qualifying monthly payments while working full time for a qualifying employer. The 120 payments do not need to be consecutive. A borrower should periodically submit employment certification and track progress in the StudentAid.gov Dashboard.

Qualifying employment and qualifying payments are separate requirements. A borrower should not assume that public-service job title, a payment made, or an employer’s nonprofit status alone proves eligibility.

## Repayment-plan implications

For borrowers with only loans disbursed before July 1, 2026, FSA describes a qualifying payment as a full, on-time monthly payment, for the billed amount, made while the borrower works full time for a qualifying employer and is in a qualifying repayment plan. Income-driven plans, including RAP, are qualifying repayment plans under the FSA guidance.

For borrowers with any loan disbursed on or after July 1, 2026 who are enrolled in RAP, FSA states that only payments made in the full amount due on or before the due date qualify for PSLF.

Payments made under the Tiered Standard Plan do not qualify for PSLF. The standard 10-year repayment path can also leave no remaining balance to forgive at 120 payments, so a borrower should compare total cost, payment amount, balance trajectory, and PSLF eligibility rather than selecting a plan based on one feature.

## SAVE transition and forbearance

SAVE ended in March 2026. Moving from SAVE to an eligible plan does not remove qualifying PSLF payments earned before the SAVE forbearance. Time spent in the SAVE forbearance does not count toward PSLF under the cited FSA guidance.

FSA states that some borrowers who already have 120 months of qualifying employment may be able to buy back certain otherwise-ineligible deferment or forbearance months, including SAVE-forbearance months. This is not automatic and must be confirmed through FSA’s PSLF process.

## Consolidation caution

Consolidation creates a new Direct Consolidation Loan, changes repayment options, and can affect how prior qualifying payments are credited. FSA advises borrowers to certify qualifying employment applicable to loans before consolidating so the weighted-average credit can be correctly applied. Review personalized results before consolidating; do not assume that consolidation preserves every benefit in the same way.
