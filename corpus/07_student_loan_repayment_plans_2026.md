# Federal Student Loan Repayment Plans — October 2026 Reference

Source: U.S. Department of Education, Federal Student Aid (FSA), “SAVE Plan Borrowers: FAQs About Choosing a Different Repayment Plan,” accessed November 16, 2026.
https://studentaid.gov/articles/save/

This is general reference material, not individualized repayment, tax, or legal advice. A borrower should confirm loan type, disbursement dates, servicer notices, repayment-plan eligibility, payment amount, and deadlines in their StudentAid.gov account and with their loan servicer.

## SAVE transition

A court order ended the Saving on a Valuable Education (SAVE) Plan in March 2026. Borrowers enrolled in SAVE, or with a pending SAVE application, generally need to choose another repayment plan for which they are eligible.

The borrower’s servicer sends the switching notice. Once that notice is received, the borrower has 90 days to choose an eligible plan. If the borrower does not act, the servicer sends a final 30-day notice. The operative deadline is based on the servicer notice, not a separate FSA email.

If no plan is selected by the final deadline, the borrower is automatically placed in a fixed-payment plan. A borrower with no loan disbursed on or after July 1, 2026 is placed in the Standard Repayment Plan. A borrower with a loan disbursed on or after July 1, 2026 that has entered repayment is placed in the Tiered Standard Plan.

## Why loan dates and loan types matter

Borrowers should check their Dashboard for both loan type and original disbursement date before comparing plans.

- For loans disbursed before July 1, 2026, fixed repayment options include Standard, Graduated, and Extended repayment.
- If a borrower has a loan disbursed on or after July 1, 2026, the fixed-payment option is Tiered Standard.
- Income-driven repayment eligibility is plan- and loan-specific. IBR, ICR, and PAYE have pre-July 1, 2026 eligibility limits and loan-type restrictions. RAP applies to eligible Direct Loans regardless of disbursement date.
- Parent PLUS loans are not eligible for the IDR plans listed in the FSA comparison table. Consolidation can change eligibility and other benefits; it creates a new loan and should be evaluated using personalized results.

For borrowers whose loans were all disbursed on or after July 1, 2026, FSA’s IDR guidance states that RAP is the available IDR plan, subject to loan-type restrictions.

## Comparing plans

The FSA Repayment Calculator can use a borrower’s account data to estimate monthly payments, total amounts paid, and potential discharge timelines. A lower payment can extend repayment and increase total interest; it may also affect the timing of income-driven repayment discharge.

Under the October 2026 FSA guidance, Tiered Standard terms depend on balance: 10 years for balances below $25,000, 15 years below $50,000, 20 years below $100,000, and 25 years above $100,000. These are reference terms, not a substitute for the borrower’s actual servicer calculation.

## RAP and pending applications

FSA describes RAP as an eligible income-driven plan for Direct Loans. Borrowers who make full, on-time monthly RAP payments that do not cover monthly accrued interest may receive an interest subsidy and may receive a matching principal payment, subject to plan rules.

A pending SAVE application is denied because SAVE ended. A borrower should use the Repayment Calculator and submit an application for another eligible plan. For a different pending IDR request, StudentAid.gov’s “My Activity” page shows its status; a servicer may use processing forbearance for up to 60 days while processing.

## Important limits

Changing plans does not erase previously qualifying PSLF payments or credited IDR payments. However, time in the SAVE forbearance does not receive PSLF or IDR-discharge credit. Questions involving consolidation, PSLF progress, default, delinquency, or a servicer dispute should be verified with FSA or the servicer before action is taken.
