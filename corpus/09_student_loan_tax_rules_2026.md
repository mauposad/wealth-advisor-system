# Student Loans and Federal Tax Rules — 2026 Reference

Sources:

- Internal Revenue Service, *Publication 970 (2025), Tax Benefits for Education*, accessed November 16, 2026. https://www.irs.gov/publications/p970
- Internal Revenue Service, “Updates to frequently asked questions about educational assistance programs,” accessed November 16, 2026. https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/updates-to-frequently-asked-questions-about-educational-assistance-programs

This is general federal-tax reference material, not tax advice. Tax treatment depends on the tax year, filing status, modified adjusted gross income (MAGI), loan facts, employer-plan design, and applicable federal and state law. Refer a taxpayer to a qualified tax professional for individualized guidance.

## Student loan interest deduction

For tax year 2025, a taxpayer may deduct the lesser of $2,500 or the qualified student-loan interest actually paid, subject to eligibility requirements and MAGI phaseout. Publication 970 lists a phaseout range of $85,000 to $100,000 for most filers and $170,000 to $200,000 for married taxpayers filing jointly. These thresholds are tax-year-specific and must be checked for the relevant filing year.

The deduction is an adjustment to income, so itemizing is not required. In general, the taxpayer must be legally obligated to pay interest on a qualified student loan, must not file married filing separately, and must not be claimable as someone else’s dependent.

Do not treat every payment connected to a student loan as deductible interest. Publication 970 excludes, among other items, interest the taxpayer is not legally obligated to pay, certain lender fees, amounts eligible for another tax benefit, and interest paid by an employer under a qualifying educational-assistance program after March 27, 2020.

## Loan cancellation and repayment assistance

As a general rule, a canceled loan or a loan payment made by someone else may be taxable income to the borrower. Publication 970 identifies circumstances in which qualifying education-loan cancellation can be excluded from income, including certain loans tied to public-service or underserved-area work. The facts and legal authority matter; cancellation should not be labeled tax-free without current tax verification.

Publication 970 notes that American Rescue Plan Act treatment for certain student-loan discharges applied to discharges from 2021 through 2025. Do not assume that temporary federal treatment applies after that period without checking current law.

Certain health-service loan repayment assistance is tax-free, including payments under the National Health Service Corps Loan Repayment Program and specified state programs for health services in underserved or shortage areas. A borrower cannot also deduct student-loan interest to the extent that those programs paid it.

## Employer educational assistance under Section 127

Under IRS Section 127 guidance, an employer’s compliant educational-assistance program can provide tax-free payments of principal or interest on a qualified education loan incurred for the employee’s own education. Payments can go to the employee or directly to a loan servicer, depending on plan design.

For calendar years through 2026, the total exclusion for qualified-loan payments and other educational assistance combined is $5,250 per calendar year. The IRS states that the limit is adjusted for inflation for tax years beginning after 2026. Unused annual exclusion is not carried forward. Benefits for a spouse or dependent do not qualify under Section 127 unless that person is also an employee.

The availability and tax treatment of an employer benefit depend on the employer’s written plan and the underlying loan. Verify benefits with the employer and current IRS guidance before relying on them.
