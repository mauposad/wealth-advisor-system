# Household Financial Context — Federal Reserve SHED 2026

Source: Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, *Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025*, Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking (SHED), published May 13, 2026.
https://www.federalreserve.gov/consumerscommunities/shed.htm
https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2026-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2025-executive-summary.htm

SHED is population-level survey evidence, not a benchmark for an individual household or a personalized recommendation. The 2025 survey was conducted in October 2025 and included nearly 13,000 adults.

## Income and expenses context

In the 2025 survey, 58% of adults said changes in the prices they paid compared with the prior year had made their financial situation worse. Sixteen percent of adults did not pay all of their bills in the prior month. Fifty-nine percent experienced at least one major unexpected expense in the prior 12 months.

These results support asking about income stability, recurring expenses, emergency reserves, other debt payments, and upcoming large obligations before discussing a debt-repayment strategy. They do not establish what a particular borrower can afford.

## Young and mid-career household considerations

For adults ages 18 to 29, 47% received help from someone outside their household to pay an expense during the prior 12 months. The most common forms of help included cell-phone bills, general expenses, and housing costs. In 2025, 49% of adults under 30 lived with a parent.

The cited SHED executive summary does not provide a single finding specifically for ages 30 to 45 in its income-and-expenses summary. For a borrower in that age range, use the report as broad household-finance context rather than claiming it reports a 30-to-45 statistic.

## Student-loan and credit context

Twenty-three percent of adults with student loans reported recent payment difficulty. Slightly more than three-quarters of borrowers reporting payment difficulty attributed it to affordability-related reasons.

When affordability is a concern, an assistant should help identify the relevant facts—loan type, current repayment plan, required payment, income, household obligations, and servicer notices—and direct the borrower to official repayment tools or a qualified human professional as appropriate. It should not assume that a survey statistic proves hardship or determines the best repayment plan for an individual.
